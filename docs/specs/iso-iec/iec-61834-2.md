# IEC 61834-2: description from free sources

A description of the parts of IEC 61834-2 (*SD format for 525-60 and 625-50 systems*, 1998), the consumer DV format, that the deliverables need, written for this project from free sources ([README](README.md)). It is not the standard.

## 1. Scope

Plan section 4.3 calls for probing raw DV streams and DV samples in AVI (types 1 and 2) and MOV. The DIF header identifies the system. The intended auxiliary-pack fields are display aspect ratio, audio sampling rate and channel count. Sections 6 and 7 and the gaps below describe the evidence available for consumer DV.

SMPTE 314M-2005 defines professional DV-based formats at 25 and 50 Mb/s. Its informative annex A, table A.1, identifies a shared DIF data structure for the 25 Mb/s format and IEC 61834. The table refers separately to IEC 61834 for the contents of consumer auxiliary packs. Sections 3 to 5 describe the SMPTE fields and codes. Section 6 gives the consumer values identified in the annex. RFC 6469 section 2.1 also describes the DIF framing common to the DV formats.

## 2. DIF blocks and sequences

Source: `smpte-st-314`, sections 4.2 and 4.3.1, tables 1 and 2. `rfc6469`, section 2.1, gives the same block structure for both formats: 80-byte blocks with a 3-byte ID that gives the block's type and position, five block types, and audio blocks of 5 bytes of auxiliary data and 72 bytes of audio.

- Each frame has 10 DIF sequences per DIF channel in the 525/60 system and 12 in the 625/50 system. Consumer SD DV and the 25 Mb/s SMPTE format use one DIF channel. The 50 Mb/s SMPTE format uses two.
- A DIF sequence has 150 DIF blocks: 1 header, 2 subcode, 3 VAUX, 9 audio and 135 video blocks.
- A DIF block is 80 bytes: a 3-byte ID (bytes 0 to 2) and 77 bytes of data (bytes 3 to 79).

The three ID bytes have the following fields, most significant first.

| Byte | Bit allocation |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Bits 7–5 are `SCT` (section type), bit 4 is reserved, and bits 3–0 are arbitrary. |
| 1 | Bits 7–4 are `Dseq` (sequence number), bit 3 is `FSC` (DIF channel), and bits 2–0 are reserved. |
| 2 | Bits 7–0 are `DBN` (block number within the section). |

The section types are: 000 header, 001 subcode, 010 VAUX (section 4.5.1), 011 audio (section 4.6.1), 100 video (section 4.7.1), and 101 to 111 reserved. Table 2 of the 2005 text prints "Audio" for 100 as well; section 4.7.1 gives 100 to the video section. Reserved bits are set to 1.

## 3. Header DIF block

Source: `smpte-st-314`, section 4.3.2, table 6. The data bytes are numbered as in the block, so byte 3 is the first byte after the ID. Bytes 8 to 79 are reserved.

| Byte | Bit 7 | Bit 6 | Bits 5 to 3 | Bits 2 to 0 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | `DSF` | 0 | reserved | reserved |
| 4 | reserved | reserved | reserved | `APT` |
| 5 | `TF1` | reserved | reserved | `AP1` |
| 6 | `TF2` | reserved | reserved | `AP2` |
| 7 | `TF3` | reserved | reserved | `AP3` |

- `DSF` is 0 for 10 DIF sequences per DIF channel (525/60) and 1 for 12 (625/50).
- `APT`, `AP1`, `AP2` and `AP3` are the application IDs of the track, the audio, the video and the subcode. In this standard they are 001 when the source is a digital VCR, and all ones when the source is unknown.
- `TF1`, `TF2` and `TF3` are the transmitting flags of the audio blocks, of the VAUX and video blocks, and of the subcode blocks: 0 when their data is valid, 1 when it is invalid.

## 4. Video auxiliary packs

Source: `smpte-st-314`, section 4.5.2, figure 7 and tables 12 to 14.

- Each VAUX DIF block holds 15 packs of 5 bytes and 2 reserved bytes (FFh). A pack is a header byte (`PC0`), which names it, and four data bytes (`PC1` to `PC4`).
- The 45 packs of a DIF sequence are numbered 0 to 44. In even DIF sequences the source pack (VS) is pack 39 and the source control pack (VSC) is pack 40; in odd ones they are packs 0 and 1.
- A pack filled with FFh carries no information.

**Source pack**, `PC0` = 0x60:

- `PC2`: `B/W` (bit 7, 1 for color), `EN` (bit 6) and `CLF` (bits 5 and 4). A zero in `EN` marks the color frame identification code `CLF` as valid.
- `PC3`: `50/60` (bit 5): 0 for the 60-field system, 1 for the 50-field system. `STYPE` (bits 4 to 0): 00000 for 4:1:1 compression, 00100 for 4:2:2.
- `PC4`: `VISC`.

**Source control pack**, `PC0` = 0x61:

- `PC1`: `CGMS` (bits 7 and 6).
- `PC2`: `DISP` (bits 2 to 0), the display mode: 000 for 4:3 full format, 010 for 16:9 full format (squeeze), others reserved.
- `PC3`: `FF` (bit 7), `FS` (bit 6), `FC` (bit 5) and `IL` (bit 4). `IL` is 1 for interlaced, 0 for noninterlaced. When `FF` is 1, both fields are delivered and `FS` identifies the first field delivered (1 for field 1, 0 for field 2). When `FF` is 0, the field identified by `FS` is delivered twice.

## 5. Audio auxiliary packs

Source: `smpte-st-314`, section 4.6.2.3, figure 10 and tables 15 to 17.

- Each audio DIF block carries one 5-byte pack in bytes 3 to 7, followed by 72 bytes of audio data. The packs of the 9 audio blocks of a DIF sequence are numbered 0 to 8.
- In even DIF sequences the source pack (AS) is pack 3 and the source control pack (ASC) is pack 4; in odd ones they are packs 0 and 1.

**Source pack**, `PC0` = 0x50:

- `PC1`: `LF` (bit 7), the locked mode (0 for locked), and `AF SIZE` (bits 5 to 0), the samples per frame. 010100 is 1600 and 010110 is 1602 in the 525/60 system; 011000 is 1920 in the 625/50 system.
- `PC2`: `CHN` (bits 6 and 5) counts the channels in one audio block (00 for one). The 25 and 50 Mb/s SMPTE formats both use 00 and carry two and four channels, respectively. `AUDIO MODE` occupies bits 3 to 0.
- `PC3`: `50/60` (bit 5), and `STYPE` (bits 4 to 0), the audio blocks per video frame: 00000 for 2, 00010 for 4.
- `PC4`: `SMP` (bits 5 to 3), the sampling frequency: 000 for 48 kHz. `QU` (bits 2 to 0), the quantization: 000 for 16-bit linear.

An audio block is 45 audio DIF blocks in five consecutive DIF sequences (525/60), or 54 in six (625/50).

**Source control pack**, `PC0` = 0x51: `CGMS`, the emphasis flag `EFC`, the recording start and end flags, and the speed.

## 6. Differences in consumer DV

The consumer entries below come from `smpte-st-314`, annex A, table A.1.

- The annex lists 000 for the consumer application IDs `APT`, `AP1`, `AP2` and `AP3` and 001 for the SMPTE digital VCR source.
- `FSC` is not defined in IEC 61834 and is set to 0.
- The sampling structure of the video is 4:1:1 in the 525/60 system and 4:2:0 in the 625/50 system.
- The audio can be 48 kHz, 44.1 kHz or 32 kHz with 16 bits and 2 channels, or 32 kHz with 12 bits and 4 channels. It can be locked or unlocked.
- The annex refers to IEC 61834 for the consumer video and audio source and source control packs. The wider ranges of audio bit depth and channel count in RFC 6469 section 2.1 cover several DV formats. The combinations in the preceding bullet are specific to consumer SD DV.

## 7. Consumer auxiliary packs

Source: `uspto-us-5907444`, Sony's patent of a digital VCR with a cassette memory (priority 1993-09-30): its figures 31, 32, 37 and 38 give the pack layouts, and its description of those figures gives the codes. The patent describes the consumer format as proposed in 1993, before IEC 61834 was published in 1998; behavior taken from it is labeled descriptive. A pack is named by its header byte `PC0`, as in SMPTE ST 314 (section 4). Bit 7 is the most significant.

**Audio source pack**, `PC0` = 0x50 (figure 31):

| Byte | Fields |
| --- | --- |
| `PC1` | `LF` (bit 7): 0 when the audio sampling frequency is locked to the video, 1 when unlocked. Bit 6 is 1. `AF SIZE` (bits 5 to 0): the number of audio samples in the frame. |
| `PC2` | The audio channel mode (bits 7 to 5): 0 for the 2-channel mode, 1 for the 4-channel mode, others reserved. `PA` (bit 4), the pair flag. `AUDIO MODE` (bits 3 to 0), the contents of each channel. |
| `PC3` | Bits 7 and 6 are 1. `50/60` (bit 5) and `STYPE` (bits 4 to 0) together tell the 50 Hz and 60 Hz HD systems and the PAL and NTSC systems apart. |
| `PC4` | `EF` (bit 7), emphasis: 0 for on, 1 for off. `TC` (bit 6), the time constant of the emphasis: 1 for 50/15 µs. `SMP` (bits 5 to 3): 0 for 48 kHz, 1 for 44.1 kHz, 2 for 32 kHz, others reserved. `QU` (bits 2 to 0): 0 for 16-bit linear, 1 for 12-bit nonlinear, others reserved. |

**Audio source control pack**, `PC0` = 0x51 (figure 32): `PC1` is reserved; `PC2` holds the recording start and end flags and the recording mode; `PC3` the direction flag `DRF` and the speed; `PC4` the genre category.

**Video source pack**, `PC0` = 0x60 (figure 37): `PC1` and the low half of `PC2` hold the television channel; `PC2` also holds `B/W` (bit 7), `EN` (bit 6) and `CLF` (bits 5 and 4); `PC3` holds the source code (bits 7 and 6), `50/60` (bit 5) and `STYPE` (bits 4 to 0); `PC4` the tuner category.

**Video source control pack**, `PC0` = 0x61 (figure 38): `PC1` is reserved. `PC2` holds the recording start flag (bit 7), the recording mode (bits 5 and 4) and the display mode `DISP` (bits 2 to 0), which indicates the aspect ratio of the display. `PC3` holds `FF`, `FS`, `FC` and `IL` (bits 7 to 4), the still image flag `ST` (bit 3), the still camera flag `SC` (bit 2) and the broadcasting system `BCSYS` (bits 1 and 0). `PC4` holds the genre category.

The positions of `STYPE`, `50/60`, `SMP`, `QU`, `DISP` and the flags of `PC3` agree with the SMPTE packs of sections 4 and 5. The consumer codes for the sampling frequency, the quantization and the channel mode are those above. They match the consumer audio modes of section 6.

## Gaps

- **Display codes.** The patent places `DISP` in `PC2` bits 2 to 0 of the video source control pack, but gives no values for it. The SMPTE values of section 4 are not established for consumer DV, so a consumer DV probe leaves the aspect ratio absent; reading it is deferred to G17.n (G1.6).
- **Pack positions.** The sources read do not give the positions of the consumer packs in the DIF sequence. The probe looks for a pack by its header byte among the VAUX and audio packs of the first DIF sequence; a pack that is not found leaves its properties absent in a partial result (plan section 4.3).
- **Unread codes.** The values of `STYPE` and `AUDIO MODE` for consumer DV are not given by the patent, and are not read.
